# ADHD-informed learner support

Use this skill to reduce avoidable executive-function load. It is not a
diagnostic tool: adapt to the requested support needs and observed learning
conditions, and do not infer ADHD from one answer, a time limit or a profile
label.

## Design contract

- State one visible goal and one next action. Chunk longer work into short
  cycles with a progress marker and a meaningful stopping point.
- Prefer active retrieval, sorting, comparing, predicting, explaining or
  manipulating over long passive exposition.
- Keep instructions short and numbered. Put essential information next to the
  action; do not make the learner remember a paragraph while working elsewhere.
- Give specific, immediate feedback tied to the work: what was done, what to
  try next and how to check it.
- Offer bounded choices rather than open-ended overload. Allow a short pause,
  movement or reset between task cycles when it does not change the objective.
- Reduce visual competition: one primary task, restrained decoration and no
  auto-advancing or distracting motion.

## Page pattern

For a short lesson, use three scenes: a fast objective and retrieval prompt;
one worked example broken into a few actions with visible progress; a new
transfer task with fewer prompts and a concise self-check. Do not equate speed
with understanding. Let the learner revisit an instruction without losing
their place.

## Safety and honesty

Do not promise a treatment effect or claim a diagnosis. If a teacher or parent
needs a longer support plan, recommend coordination with the learner's school
team or qualified professionals rather than expanding one generated lesson
into a clinical recommendation.
